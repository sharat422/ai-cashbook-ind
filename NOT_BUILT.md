# Not built yet

Things that were asked for, or came up, but are not in the code. Delete a line when it ships.

## 0. Open questions — decisions needed (November 2026)

Each one is built the way described below until someone decides otherwise.

1. **12% for bricks and tiles: fix now or after the CA confirms?** Notification 14/2025 keeps 12% from 22 Sep 2025 for building bricks, fly ash bricks/blocks/aggregates and earthen/roofing tiles (HSN 6815, 6904, 6905); the code refuses it (§3). The fix is small (allow 12% after that date only for those HSN prefixes). G1 says build nothing in GST before the CA test. Decide: fix before the pilot, or pick a pilot that doesn't sell these.
2. **The 6% brick scheme.** Some sources mention a 6% option for bricks with no input tax credit (Notification 02/2022-CT(Rate)). The app has no 6% rate. Ask the CA whether any pilot distributor uses it.
3. **Daily close difference counts cash until it's banked (R3).** Since R3, "Difference to explain" includes cash until it's deposited and matched, or recorded as kept back, so a day with cash collections is never 0.00 until then. The alternative is to leave cash out of the difference, as before R3, and show deposits separately. Keep it as is?
4. **Staff see their own count only (B6).** With per-person counts, staff see their own counted cash and over/short but not the beat's total counted or over/short, because that would reveal a colleague's short. The spec only said "own row". Should staff see the beat total?
5. **Staff see their own deposits only (R10).** Same rule for deposits: staff see whole-beat deposits and their own, not a colleague's. OK?
6. **"Helpers" or "Also on this beat" (B4).** The B4 spec calls the section "Helpers"; the app still says "Also on this beat" so the reviewed wording wasn't changed. Rename (and retranslate)?
7. **Helper dates show "16 Nov 2026", not "Mon 16 Nov" (B5).** The app has no weekday date format. Add one?
8. **Where the CA's rate confirmation is recorded (G3).** The G3 prompt says "the rate-change log", but that log (`gst_rate_change_logs`) records a business changing one item's rate and has no field for a CA's name or a confirmation of the overall list. For now the confirmation goes in docs/CA_TALLY_TEST.md (signed), RELEASE_NOTES.md, and a comment next to the rates in `backend/app/gst.py`. Want it stored in the app instead (a small table: CA name, date, list version)?
9. **Is an item's sale price with or without GST? (G5)** The GST sale amount is pre-filled from the item's sale price × quantity and taxed the way the "price includes GST" switch says (default: no, tax added on top). If distributors store sale prices including GST (MRP-style), the default adds tax twice unless the owner flips the switch. Should picking an item set the switch, and from what (a per-item or per-business setting)? Ask the CA in G1.
10. **How the G6 backfill decides an old invoice's item.** Old GST sales don't store the item, so the script treats the item as known only when exactly one catalogue product has the same HSN and GST rate, and skips the rest. Looser matching (by name in the notes, or the item with that HSN sold most often) would fill more lines but could guess wrong. Keep it strict?
11. **Cash GST sales and the daily close (G7).** As the G7 spec says, a cash GST sale is left out of the daily close: it isn't credit given or a collection. But a salesperson who takes that cash on a beat is holding it, so counted cash will show over by that amount. Should cash GST sales count as cash in hand in the daily close instead?
12d. **STOP from a retailer of two distributors (ST2).** Server sends all come from one Bolcash number. A STOP that quotes a statement opts out of that distributor. A STOP typed on its own opts out of the distributor who sent last. Should it opt out of every distributor that has sent them a statement from that number instead? That's stricter on consent, but it stops statements the retailer may still want.
12c. ~~**Printing a bill without WhatsApp consent (SC6).**~~ Answered by ST3: a bill (or statement) can always be emailed or saved and then printed. That's logged but not consent-gated; only WhatsApp and the open share sheet need the opt-in.
12b. **An item bill dated in a locked month is refused (S8).** S8 locks stock only, not sales. An item bill is both a sale and a stock movement, so a bill dated in a locked month is refused (its stock can't move). A credit entry with no items still saves. Keep it that way, or let such a bill save without moving stock?
12a. **Reversing a purchase leaves its expense (S4 vs S3).** S3 books a purchase's expense in the same transaction so stock and expenses never disagree; S4 reverses only the stock (as it says for a sale's credit). After reversing a purchase, Expenses still shows it. Should a purchase reversal also post a negative expense (expenses are positive today), or should the owner correct it by hand?
12. **Deleting a customer deletes their GST invoices (G8 point 4).** G8 says never hard-delete an issued invoice. Today the only way an invoice goes is deleting its customer: the number stays as cancelled (Table 13) and any credit notes keep their own copy, but the invoice row is gone. Refuse deleting a customer who has GST invoices (they'd stay, hidden), or keep it as is?
13. **When a confirmed ITC reversal counts in GSTR-3B (G8).** A note reduces the 3B summary of the period it's dated in, once confirmed. If the buyer confirms in a later month, should the reduction move to that month instead? Ask the CA.
14. **The credit-note law in G8.** Built as the G8 spec states it (s.34, the proviso to s.34(2) from 1 Oct 2025, s.15(3)(b) from 1 Nov 2026, the 30 Nov deadline). Have the CA confirm, including whether unregistered B2C small notes should be netted off b2cs (as built) or listed in cdnur.
15. **Daily close after restoring a statement (R5).** Restored lines come back unmatched, so the difference stays at its after-delete value until the owner re-confirms the matches; the R5 spec said "back to its pre-delete value". Keep as is (no silent re-match)?

## 4. Stock basics: gaps left (updated November 2026)

Built: item catalog as the SKU master, godown and per-salesperson van stock, purchases (with supplier, price per unit and the supplier's bill number; the item keeps the latest price), sales from the godown or a van, godown → van loads, the low-stock list, the monthly count with logged corrections, stock history, and stock in "Download my data". Stock can't be oversold: taking stock out is one conditional database update, backed by a CHECK (qty ≥ 0) and a 20-sales-at-once test. Vans and people show the name the owner gives them. See MANUAL_TESTING.md §37D and §37M. **Out of scope by design (Phase 1):** warehouse/bin management, batches and expiry, manufacturing.

These parts are not built yet:

- **Add credit and income don't reduce stock — by design (S1), never build.** A plain amount has no items, so reducing stock would mean inventing which items left. A sale with items is an item bill (§5), which takes the goods out of stock. Refuse any prompt that deducts stock from a bare amount.
- **Stock moves (S2): built.** Stock → Move: from the godown or a van, to the godown or another van (`POST /stock/transfers` with from/to; the same code and pair of history rows as a godown → van load, kind "transfer" — not a separate `stock_transfers` table). The source gives only what it has (one conditional UPDATE + CHECK qty ≥ 0); total stock never changes. Staff move only out of their own van, and on their screen only back to the godown (they don't get the team list; the owner does van → van). Not built: several items in one move.
- **Suppliers and purchase expenses (S3): built.** A purchase picks its supplier from the list (`GET/POST /suppliers`) or adds a typed name to it (same spelling = case and spaces ignored; anything else is a new supplier). Each purchase books an expense (category Purchases, quantity × price + the bill's GST, `purchase_id` = the purchase) in the same transaction as the stock: both or neither. `scripts/backfill_suppliers.py` (run once after deploy) builds the list from old purchases' typed names and never merges two spellings. Not built: merging or editing suppliers (the owner can't yet combine "Sharma Trader" into "Sharma Traders"), expenses for purchases saved before this release (the backfill doesn't create them), and a supplier screen of its own (the list shows on the purchase sheet).
- **Undoing a stock entry (S4): built, by reversal.** Stock history → **Reverse** (or `POST /stock/moves/{id}/reverse`): a mirror entry (same item and place, opposite quantity, `reverses_id`) undoes it; the original stays, marked Reversed. Once only; counts and reversal rows can't be reversed; a transfer is reversed as a pair; refused if the stock is no longer there. Staff only on their own van; accountants never. There's no edit endpoint. Not built: Reverse on the staff van screen (staff can use the API; the owner's history screen has the button), and reversing a sale's credit entry or a purchase's expense with it — those stay and are corrected separately (see §0).
- **Offline stock (S5): built.** Stock moves (purchases, sales) and transfers join the phone's offline queues (`stockQueue.ts`, persisted like visits; synced on reconnect, on foreground and by "Sync now"; shown on Sync status). Each carries its client id, and the server keeps one row per id, so a retry after a lost response adds nothing. A queued sale or move the place no longer has the stock for becomes a conflict (Sync status → "Stock entries waiting for you": recorded vs in stock now, Try again / Discard) — never an oversell, never dropped. Counts are online-only: the count screen says so and refuses offline. Not built: reversals (S4) offline, and showing a queued sale in the van's stock before it syncs (the van list updates after sync).
- **Units (S6): built for stock.** An item's `unit` is its base unit (stock is always held in it); `item_units` adds others as a number of base units (box = 24 pcs) via `PUT /items/{id}/units`, locked (422) once the item has any purchase, sale, transfer or count — the fix is a new item. Stock purchases, sales and transfers take a `unit`, are converted to base before the stock check, and keep what was entered (history shows "240 (10 box)"); a purchase price is per the unit bought in. The stock list reads "8 box 18 pcs"; the stock sheet has unit chips. Not built: a screen to set an item's units (API only), units on item bills (bills stay in the base unit, because scheme slabs count base quantities), counts in other units, and the GST line UQC from the line's unit (GST invoices don't move stock, and multi-line invoices — G4 — aren't built).
- **Low-stock notifications (S7): deferred on purpose — don't build yet.** The list stays on the Stock screen. A push is only as good as the stock figure behind it; revisit after one full month of counts that match physical stock within ±2% (S8), and then only as one daily digest, not one message per item.
- **Month-end count reminder and lock (S8): built.** Owner's home screen: "Month-end count due" on the last 3 days of the month until that month is confirmed (a card, not a push). Stock → Count → **Confirm count and lock month** (needs a count dated in the month): after that, any stock entry dated in it — a purchase, sale, transfer, count, reversal or item bill — is refused with "Month locked — add it to the open month". Sales and payments (the ledger) are not locked. **Unlock month** is owner-only, writes an audit-log row, and the count screen shows who unlocked it and when. Not built: locking several months at once, and a list of past locks (the count screen shows the month of its date).
- **Stock value (S9): deferred on purpose.** No statutory valuation: the method is the CA's call, and AS 2 / Ind AS 2 allow FIFO or weighted average only — LIFO is prohibited; never build or accept LIFO. Later, once a full month of purchases with prices exists: an owner's stock-value report using weighted average (total purchase cost ÷ total quantity per item), saying on screen which method it uses — a report for the owner, not a filing.
- **Existing oversold stock (S10): handled, no build.** If the old ledger already summed below zero somewhere, that place starts at 0 (logged at boot); the next count corrects it. Keep the boot log, and never backfill fake positive stock to hide it.
- **Translations (S11).** The `stock.*` strings are machine-translated; a native reviewer (not the founder) does sheet 3 of the review files. Process, not code.

## 5. Item bills and FMCG schemes: gaps after the first version (November 2026)

Built: item bills to a retailer (godown or van) that post the total to the retailer's credit and take paid and free goods out of stock; a scheme master (10+1, quantity slab, % off, free goods) with SKUs, dates and priority; automatic application at billing with the scheme as its own line; the "not applied" reason when a lower-priority scheme loses; the scheme log per bill and per retailer; and the scheme report by scheme, SKU and month. See MANUAL_TESTING.md §37E. **Out of scope by design (Phase 1):** scheme claim filing and distributor claim automation.

These parts are not built yet:

- **BLOCKED — GST on item bills (SC1): needs the CA's written ruling first.** Item bills are not GST invoices: not in GSTR-1 or the Tally exports (`test_item_bills_not_gst.py` holds that). The CA rules per scheme type — free goods (Circular 92/11/2019-GST para 2(B): tied free goods aren't a gift, tax on the price collected, ITC kept), % off (s.15(3)(a) at supply vs a GST credit note under the new s.15(3)(b) from 1 Nov 2026), and free samples/gifts (s.17(5)(h) ITC block; the 57th Council's proposal to remove it isn't law as of Nov 2026). Questions and the ruling form: docs/CA_TALLY_TEST.md, "Ruling: GST on item bills and schemes". Only then write the build prompt, quoting the ruling.

- **Mixed-SKU (group) schemes (SC2): built.** A scheme's scope is "each SKU on its own" (as before) or "these SKUs together": the group's lines on a bill are added up (6 A + 4 B = 10 for "any 10"). Free goods go on the cheapest line; a % off or slab applies to every line in the group. Schemes still go in priority order, one per line: a line a higher-priority scheme took doesn't count towards a group. The bill and the scheme log show "6 Soap A + 4 Soap B = 10 · free: Soap B". Not built: groups that aren't the scheme's own SKU list (e.g. a named brand family shared by several schemes), and splitting free goods across lines.
- **Bill-level schemes (SC3): built.** A scheme applies to each bill line (as before) or the whole bill: ₹ off or % off on bills of at least a set value, for all retailers or one. Checked once per bill, after the line schemes, on what's left; one per bill — the lowest priority number wins and the others show "not applied" with the reason; never stacked. The bill shows it as its own "Whole bill" line; the scheme log and report carry it. Not built: bill schemes on free goods (e.g. "above ₹10,000, a free carton"), and a bill scheme for a group of retailers (one, or all).
- **Stacking — not now, on purpose (SC4).** Exactly one scheme per line, and one bill scheme per bill; the loser shows "not applied". Two discounts on one line raise whether the second applies to the discounted or original price, which changes the tax — revisit only with the CA's ruling on combined discounts (SC1).
- **Cancelling or returning a bill (SC5): built, by reversal.** Bill → **Cancel bill** or **Return lines** (owner; `POST /bills/{id}/cancel`): a return bill (lines negated, `reverses_id` = the original) puts the goods — and those lines' free goods — back where they came from, and a "bill return" entry lowers what the retailer owes by the lines' net amount (less their line discount and their share of any bill discount). The original stays, marked Cancelled or Partly returned; a returned line can't be returned again. The scheme log gets "reversed" rows and the scheme report nets them out. There's no bill edit. Not built: returning part of a line (a whole line goes back), and returning to a different place than it came from. The retailer's ledger shows the return as its own entry; the spec's "negative credit" is a positive "bill return" entry, because every amount in the ledger is positive and the type gives the sign.
- **Changing the rate on a bill (BL8): built.** The owner's bill screen has a rate box on each line, filled in with the item's sale price. A changed rate goes to the preview, so the line, the schemes and the total are re-worked before saving. It's saved as typed, and the line shows "Rate changed (not the catalogue price)" on the bill screen and on the saved bill. Typing the sale price back counts as unchanged. The request didn't change (`rate` per line was already accepted). The server now stores `bill_lines.rate_changed` (a startup migration) and returns `rateChanged`, because otherwise a later catalogue price change would make old bills look changed. ## 8. Khata import and low-end/offline: what is not done (November 2026)

Built: import from Excel/CSV with column mapping (date, retailer, bill no., amount, udhar/jama, opening balance), headings recognised in English, Telugu and Hindi; a preview that is a dry run; duplicate detection (same retailer + bill no. + amount + date, against the books and within the file) so a re-import adds nothing; an import log (date, file, imported, skipped, who) and an encrypted backup of the khata taken before each import, downloadable from the log. Offline: income, expense, udhar/payment and visit queues sync exactly once — the database now refuses a second row for the same client id, and overlapping syncs share one run. One sync status (synced / waiting / failed / conflict) on home and a Sync status screen; customer edits made offline are queued; a customer changed on two devices goes to a side-by-side conflict screen and waits for a choice. Logging out warns before deleting unsynced entries. See MANUAL_TESTING.md §37G–37H.

These parts are not done:

- **BLOCKED — low-end performance target device.** "[LOW-END TEST DEVICE AND ANDROID VERSION]" was not named, so the on-device timings (open, first offline entry under 2 s, 1,000-entry scroll) have not been measured. What was done: offline saves no longer wait for a network timeout; the ledger list mounts about a screenful of rows; and the payment-risk calculation on the customer screen went from O(credits × payments) to linear (1,000 entries: ~350 ms → a few ms on a laptop) — that loop would have frozen a low-end phone for seconds. Run §37H.5 on the device once it is chosen. **The fix is a decision (IM1):** name one device and Android version, the cheapest phone a pilot merchant actually uses, not a mid-range phone from the office. Record the numbers there; if it fails, measure the fix against that device, not a laptop.
- **Weak signal, not offline (IM2): built; the on-device check is open.** Before a save, the phone asks the server a 2-second question (`GET /health`: no login, no ledger data). If there's no answer, the entry is queued at once and the real request isn't tried, so a godown with bars but no data queues in about 2 s instead of 15. If the check answers but the real request then fails, it queues as before. Airplane mode still queues at once, with no check. This covers every save that used to wait: income, udhar/payment, visits, stock moves and transfers, item bills and customer edits. Expenses always queued at once. The same queues as before; entries queued this way show **"Queued — weak signal"** on Sync status. While the signal is weak, the phone checks again every 30 s; when the server answers, the queues sync as on a reconnect, each entry exactly once (client ids). The check adds one tiny request per save and never gates syncing. **On-device acceptance (MANUAL_ONLY_TESTS group 5) isn't run yet:** a connection that accepts but never answers, airplane mode, and sync once on return. The automated tests simulate them with a fake network (`weakSignal.test.tsx`).
- **Conflicts cover customer details, credit limits and beats (IM3); the rest is last-write-wins by design.** Customer edits get the side-by-side screen; a stale credit-limit save is refused and the server's limit is shown with a notice (old phone-only limits never overwrite a newer one). Entries, payments and visits are never edited in place, so they can't conflict.
  - **Beats** now carry a version, like credit limits, and the check is atomic: one conditional update, so two phones saving at once can't both pass. A details edit or a shop-order save is sent with the version it started from. If the beat was saved on another phone since, nothing is saved: 409 `beat_changed` with the current beat. The app closes the editor, shows "<beat>: changed on another phone — review and save again", and the list shows the current beat. Adding or ending a salesperson or helper also bumps the version, so a stale details edit can't put the old salesperson back. A server counter, never a device clock. An older app that sends no version saves as before.
  - **Items, schemes and team settings stay last-write-wins, by decision.** They're settings the owner changes rarely, from one phone. A comment on each update route says so, so it isn't "fixed" by accident.
- **Who resolves a conflict: the owner, on the phone where it appeared. This is the rule, not a gap (IM4).** The owner (the only role that can edit a customer) chooses there; others see the conflict but can't choose. The conflict lives on that phone, not on the server. Don't "fix" this by moving conflicts to the server: a server-side conflict queue would let a staff member resolve an owner-only decision, and a conflict on a phone the owner has lost is a lost-phone problem, not a sync problem. If the owner loses that phone, they re-enter the change from the server's copy. That's in the Help screen (a new question) and in the assistant's backup guide; nothing is built for it.
- **Failed entries: Edit or Discard, not only retry (IM5): built.** A failed entry on Sync status (the server refused it) now has **Edit** and **Discard**.
  - **Edit** is for income, expense and udhar/payment: amount, date, invoice number or UTR, and notes. It changes the entry where it waits in its queue, keeping the same client id. The next sync creates it exactly once, because the server dedupes on that id.
  - **Discard** is owner only; staff and accountants get 403, and staff don't see the button. The confirm names the entry, and the button names the amount ("Discard ₹10,000"). It needs a connection: `POST /sync/discard` first records the client id and writes one audit row (`access_logs`, action `discard`). Only then does the entry leave the phone.
  - **Safeguards:** a discard never deletes a server row. An entry that already reached the server is refused (409 `already_synced`) and clears on the next sync. Every create path (income, expense, udhar/payment, visit, stock, bill) refuses a discarded client id (410 `entry_discarded`), so a late retry can't bring it back.
  - **Not built:** Edit for visits, stock moves and bills (their failures, such as a beat no longer assigned or short stock, aren't fixed by editing; bills and short stock already have the conflict screen), and Edit or Discard for a failed customer edit (it has no client id; it keeps retry and the conflict screen). The discard audit row is in the same table as the beat-restore and month-unlock rows; the Team → activity screen still lists only accountants' actions, as before.
- **Failed entries can only be retried**, not edited or discarded, from the Sync status screen.
- **Import limits: by design (IM6).** One sheet (the first), 10,000 rows and 5 MB per file; old .xls is read too (same reader as bank statements); PDF is refused. Mobile numbers, GSTIN and notes are not imported (new retailers are created by name). A retailer name shared by two existing customers is skipped, not guessed. Rows with no bill number still count as duplicates when the retailer, amount and date match — the preview lists them, so check it. These are the right limits for a phone import; raising them would mean importing a 5 MB file on a low-end phone. The import screen now says so before a file is picked: "First sheet only · up to 10,000 rows · 5 MB · Excel or CSV".
- **Duplicate client ids (IM8): a production runbook, not code; not run yet.** If old rows share a client id, the boot migration skips that table's unique index (logged) and the look-up-first check is the only guard until they're cleaned. The index migration isn't on `main` yet, so production has never tried it. The cleanup steps (read-only SQL report, hand merges, restart, record) are in RELEASE_NOTES.md as a one-off for the first production deploy; `scripts/reconcile.py`, which the plan named, doesn't exist. **The look-up-first check stays, by decision:** it isn't dead code once the index applies. A retried entry (a lost response, or the offline queue sending again) gets the saved row back before the route does any other work. Without it, a retried UPI payment would hit the duplicate-UTR check against itself and be refused (409); the phone would then mark a saved payment as failed. An attachment would also be stored twice. The index, plus each route's `IntegrityError` catch, only covers two requests arriving at the same instant.
- **One-tap undo of an import (IM7): built.** Past imports gain **Undo this import** (owner only; staff and accountants get 403).
  - **What it removes:** exactly what that import added, found by the import id now saved on each row: its ledger rows, and the retailers it created. The balances of retailers that were already there are recomputed. That puts the books back as in the backup taken before the import (which stays downloadable) for everything the import touched. One audit row (`access_logs`, action `undo`); the log shows "Undone on …".
  - **Never deletes later work:** undo is refused (422 `import_undo_blocked`), naming each blocker, while something done since rests on the import. That means a newer import that isn't undone (it may have skipped rows as duplicates of this one; undo newest first), an imported row something now points at (a bank match, a credit note, a UTR, a GST number), or a retailer it created that was edited since (details, credit limit) or used since (an entry, beat, bill, visit, statement). The check is generic, so a table added later is covered. The log shows the reasons instead of the button.
  - **Undone twice:** refused (422 `import_already_undone`, with the date).
  - **Imports from before this change:** their ledger rows get the import id from their client id (a startup migration), so undo removes them. The retailers they created can't be told apart, so those stay, with no entries.
  - **Not built:** undoing part of an import, and redoing an undone one (import the file again; duplicates are still skipped).
- **Existing duplicate client ids.** If the production database already has two rows with one client id (see `scripts/reconcile.py`), the new unique index for that table is skipped at boot (logged) and the old look-up-first check still applies there until the duplicates are cleaned.

## 9. Accountant read-only access: what is not done (November 2026)
